A2 homework model vs pretrained OLMo-2-0425-1B (Task 3.3)
==========================================================

A2: 2-layer Transformer, 50k word vocab, Wikipedia paragraphs, ~28M params.
OLMo: 1B-parameter base LM (not instruction-tuned), subword tokenizer.
A2 numbers from generate_a2.py; OLMo from compare_olmo_a2.txt (CPU, seed 0).

Next-word / next-token
----------------------
Prompt                         A2 argmax          OLMo argmax
She lives in San               francisco (0.41)   Francisco (0.41)
The capital of Sweden is       the (0.10)         Stockholm (0.86)
The cat sat on the             <UNK> (0.06)       sofa (0.09)

Both predict San → Francisco. OLMo also knows Sweden → Stockholm;
A2 falls back to generic Wikipedia-style "the".

Generation (greedy, 40 tokens)
------------------------------
"In natural language processing, a Transformer"
  A2:  "... a transformer and a <UNK> data structure is a <UNK> data
        structure that the data structure of data structure ..."
  OLMo: "... is a type of neural network architecture that is used for
        natural language processing tasks. It was developed by Google
        Brain researchers in 2017 ..."

"Is Stockholm the capital of Sweden? Answer yes or no. The answer is"
  A2:  "... the same . . . . . ."  (repeats periods)
  OLMo greedy: "yes."
  OLMo sample: "no."  (base LM is not a reliable QA model)

"Write a Python program that reverses a list."
  A2:  often stops immediately after the prompt (treats "." as EOS)
  OLMo: continues in English ("The list should be reversed in place...")
        — not actual Python. Expected: this OLMo checkpoint is a
        language model, not instruction-tuned.

"She lives in San"
  A2 greedy: "francisco , new york city , new york city , ..."
  OLMo greedy: "Francisco, California."

What temperature / top-k do
---------------------------
Lower T + small top-k: safer, more repetitive (A2 loops; OLMo stays on topic).
Higher T + larger top-k: more variety, more errors (OLMo: San Antonio
instead of San Francisco; A2: more UNK and topic drift).

Takeaway for the assignment
---------------------------
The homework model produces vaguely English Wikipedia-like text but
repeats and lacks facts/code. Pretrained OLMo is far more fluent and
factual on encyclopedic prompts, but still not a chatbot: sampling can
flip yes/no, and a "write a program" prompt is completed as prose.
